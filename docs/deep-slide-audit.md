# Deep audit of the UniPreneur deck

Reviewed 24 September 2026 against all six user-supplied files, the current 13-slide PowerPoint and its speaker notes, rendered pages from each reference PDF, the Rentify architecture documents, and the linked market/competitor sources. This is an editorial audit; it does not turn an untested business assumption into evidence.

## Slide-by-slide audit of the delivered draft

| **3 — Solution** | Correctly puts a Store and canonical product/price/stock/order state at the center; matches the target architecture. | The horizontal rule does not actually explain how the three channels connect. | **4 — Product proof** | Uses screenshots from the current local storefront and marketplace pilot rather than imaginary UI. Labels development status. | Screens are too small to read from the back of a room; POS is named but not shown; hosted storefront products were not visible in the checked page. This cannot demonstrate a completed three-channel transaction. | **Highest:** enlarge the evidence, state exactly what each screen proves, and reserve an actual working cross-channel recording for later if available. |
| **5 — Market** | $1.51B 2024 e-commerce figure and 2022 census count have source years and caveats. | The large 753,670 number includes all economic establishments, so it looks more addressable than it is. | **6 — Competition** | Fairly names Khmer24, Shopify and Khmum, avoiding the old deck's false monopoly claim. | “Simpler and more affordable” is a testable hypothesis, not a demonstrated edge. | **8 — Pilot plan** | Honest that traction is unverified and specifies ten pilot merchants and measurement areas. | “Recruit retailers” lacks the first acquisition channel, which the workshop explicitly emphasizes. | **9 — Team and ask** | Uses the five confirmed portraits and ends with a specific request for pilot introductions. | Portraits and names are small, and the ask is visually secondary. | **10 — Market appendix** | Separates published context from revenue. | The formula mixes a merchant count with total marketplace GMV without defining eligible volume, annual period or adoption. It describes potential revenue, not a defensible calculated TAM. | **High:** explain TAM, SAM and SOM variables separately; do not supply false totals. |
| **11 — Competitor appendix** | An editable table and source caveat; Khmum included. | Text is only 19 pt; the generic “must prove” column could be more directly tied to merchant tests. | **12 — Revenue appendix** | Arithmetic is correct and assumptions are visibly unvalidated. | The $8/3% scenario may be remembered as proposed pricing despite the caveat. | **13 — Status appendix** | Separates build from full-product validation. | Visually sparse and too abstract for a difficult Q&A. It should say which release gates are open: end-to-end stock, merchant adoption, production payments/settlement. | **High:** replace generic lists with specific proof gates and readable labels. |

## Overall decision

The draft has an honest strategic spine, but it is **not yet the strongest version for the contest**. The most useful changes are visual product proof, a more focused first customer, a precise acquisition test and a clearer closing ask. The missing merchant validation and financial inputs cannot be solved by slide design; they are work for the team before the competition. The deck should make that distinction plain.

## Revision completed after the audit

- **Slides 1–3:** the cover names Cambodian merchants; slide 2 now uses an AI-generated illustration labeled as such; slide 3 shows branches from one Store to each channel and its notes explicitly call this the intended workflow.
- **Slide 4:** the current revision shows the hosted storefront at port 4600, the team's actual marketplace home at port 4500, and the team's POS marketing preview from port 4200. The merchant POS route itself is in the dashboard at port 4400. The captions and notes distinguish the preview from a completed cross-channel transaction. A working transaction video remains an opportunity, not evidence in this deck.
- **Slides 5–6:** the first merchant segment is now the main message; the broad 2022 establishment count no longer masquerades as a customer count. The competitive claim is a test of setup, stock and cost rather than a claim of exclusivity.
- **Slides 7–9:** the revenue model names the two value exchanges, the pilot slide names a proposed recruitment route, and the final ask is visually dominant.
- **Slides 10 and 13:** the appendix separates TAM/SAM/SOM inputs and concrete release/validation gates. Slides 11–12 remain source-qualified competitor evidence and illustrative revenue arithmetic.

The revised deck is **stronger and suitable as a rehearsable draft**, but “absolute best” is not verifiable yet. Three things would materially change the competition case: real merchant discovery, a recorded working cross-channel transaction, and a bottom-up market/three-year financial model using validated prices and costs. The team should replace the relevant slides when those facts exist.

## External source check

- The Ministry of Commerce PDF linked in the first draft is a bulletin bundle whose web text extraction is unreliable. The $1.51B 2024 figure is also stated in the [U.S. International Trade Administration's Cambodia e-commerce guide](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/cambodia-ecommerce) and the [Ministry of Commerce infographic search index](https://s2.moc.gov.kh/mocspace/mocspace_1744030861626.pdf). Keep the year and definition visible.
- The [2022 Economic Census](https://nis.gov.kh/nis/EC2022/Leaflet.pdf) supports 753,670 *all establishments*; it does not identify Rentify's eligible retailer count.
- [Shopify's POS page](https://www.shopify.com/pos) explicitly describes online/in-store inventory synchronization. [Khmum's public profile](https://www.khmersme.gov.kh/service_provider/khmum-technology-co-ltd/) explicitly describes marketplace, merchant tools and hybrid POS. Those are substantive overlaps, so Rentify must prove a specific merchant advantage.
